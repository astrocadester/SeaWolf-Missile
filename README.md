# seawolf-missile
This project is a byte-exact reconstruction and reverse-engineering baseline for Sea Wolf / Missile released by Dave Nutting Associates (DNA) / Bally in 1978. This was a two-in-one game pack for the Bally Professional Arcade / Astrocade.

![Sea Wolf / Missile Box](images/seawolf-missile-catalog.png)

The original game was designed and programmed by:
- Dave Nutting (Game Designer)
- Rickey Spiece (Developer)

## Sea Wolf
This side is a port of Midway's iconic 1976 arcade shooter. You take command of a submarine at the bottom of the screen, looking upward through a "periscope" field of view.
- Gameplay: Your goal is to fire torpedoes upward to sink enemy PT boats, destroyers, and high-value freighters crossing the sea lanes. Mines drift horizontally across the middle of the screen, acting as obstacles that absorb your shots if you aren't careful.
- Controls: The port takes excellent advantage of the Astrocade's controller knob. Turning the dial smoothly pans your targeting crosshairs left and right across the horizon, while squeezing the trigger unleashes your torpedoes.

## Missile
This game is a distinct adaptation of Midway’s 1977 arcade game Guided Missile. Rather than being a defensive game like Atari's Missile Command, this is a pure offensive anti-aircraft shooter. 
- Gameplay: You control a land-based anti-air missile launcher aiming at targets flying across the upper sky.
- Twist: Unlike the arcade version—where missiles would drop back to earth if they missed—the Astrocade port requires you to actively guide the missile on its trajectory after launch. Furthermore, you have the tactical advantage of moving your base vehicle left and right along the bottom of the screen before firing your shot.

## Bombardier
 Bally originally published the two-in-one combat shooter package as Sea Wolf / Missile in 1977. When they re-issued the cartridge under a fresh production batch, they retitled it to Sea Wolf / Bombardier.
 ### Rarity
 Because the Bombardier variant was printed in much smaller quantities later in the console's life cycle, it is significantly rarer and more sought after by collectors than the standard Missile labeled cartridge
